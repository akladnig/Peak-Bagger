## Problem

Latest Walk formats its `startDateTime` without converting the timestamp to the Mac's local timezone, so the displayed walk date can be a day early or late. The separate `Track date` is a calendar-day value and must not be substituted for this timestamp display. [L1] [L2]

The importer currently derives `trackDate` using the importing Mac's timezone, while `PeaksBagged.date` separately uses Australian Eastern timezone rules. Neither reliably identifies the walk's calendar day at its recorded location. Existing records need recalculation as well as corrected future imports; the corrected day must survive persistence and changes to the Mac's timezone. [R1]

The shared Peaks Bagged, Elevation, and Distance timelines build only recent daily history for Week and Month, use half-viewport movement, and do not consistently align weekly buckets with Monday. This prevents reliable access to older records and produces partial rather than complete calendar-period navigation. Generic arrow tooltips and the absence of first/last controls make the destinations less clear. [L1] [L3] [L4]

## Proposed Outcome

Latest Walk displays the locally converted start date. The three timeline cards support contiguous, complete calendar pages throughout recorded history, Monday-based weeks, first/last record-page destinations, precise tooltips, and identical navigation semantics for buttons and direct horizontal browsing. Each settled page has one consistent chart, date range, total, and average. Keep the expanded toolbar on one row. [L1]–[L10]

Existing and future Tracks use the calendar day at the walk's location, resolved with the timezone rules applicable at its recorded timestamp, and linked peak-ascent dates agree. An automatic, versioned recalculation processes each existing database once, visibly reports its outcome, and remains safely retryable after interruption or failure. Unresolvable existing dates are retained and reported; unresolvable future imports have an unknown date and a visible warning. [R1] [R2] [R3]

## User Stories

1. As a walker, I can read Latest Walk's date in my Mac's local timezone without changing the imported walk's separate Track date.
2. As a Dashboard user, I can browse every recorded week or month with predictable Monday-based weeks and complete calendar months.
3. As a Dashboard user, I can browse complete 3-, 6-, and 12-month pages and understand how edge weeks contribute to their totals.
4. As a Dashboard user, I can jump to the pages containing my earliest/latest recorded activity and still move forwards through empty periods to the current period.
5. As a Dashboard user, I can use buttons or horizontal panning consistently, inspect weekday-bearing Month hover dates, and change chart modes without losing my page.
6. As a walker, I can import a walk anywhere and see its location-derived Track date and peak-ascent dates consistently, regardless of my Mac's timezone.
7. As a returning user, I can have existing walk dates recalculated automatically, understand updated/unchanged/skipped results, and recover safely from an interrupted recalculation.

## Requirements

### Latest Walk date

1. Select and order Latest Walk records by the existing `startDateTime` rules, including the current track-ID tie-break, null filtering, geometry/empty behavior, and track-to-map navigation. Before displaying the selected timestamp's date, convert it to the Mac's local timezone. Apply this to every walk reached through the card's existing previous/next walk controls. [L2]
2. Preserve `formatTrackDateShortMonth`'s visible format, for example `Thu, 15 Oct 2026`, and its `Unknown` behavior when formatting an absent timestamp directly. A start at `2026-10-14T23:30:00Z` displays `Thu, 15 Oct 2026` on a Mac in Hobart. This timestamp-display operation does not mutate stored timestamps, `trackDate`, or GPX content. The separately approved location-derived Track-date/import/recalculation requirements below do not replace Latest Walk's Mac-local timestamp display with `trackDate`. [L2] [R1]

### Timeline periods and page sequence

3. Apply the following consistently to **Peaks Bagged**, **Elevation**, and **Distance**, in both line and column modes. Retain the existing preset labels and ordering: `Week`, `Month`, `Last 3 Months`, `Last 6 Months`, `Last 12 Months`, `Year to Date`, `All Time`. Retain the default preset `Last 12 Months`. The default Last 12 Months likewise initially opens its current-period page. Multi-month pages are anchored to the current calendar month; the latest 3-/6-/12-month page includes that month and the preceding 2/5/11 months. Navigation changes a page in this sequence, not the current-date reference itself; calendar rollover is reconciled under requirement 23. [L3] [L4] [L7] [L10]

Calendar periods must remain correct across month/year boundaries, leap years, and daylight-saving transitions. [L1] [L3] [L6]
6. In October 2026, Last 3 Months pages are August–October, May–July, February–April, and so on. If the latest record is in June, last opens May–July, not April–June. ### Monday-based weeks and summaries

8. All weekly grouping uses Monday–Sunday. In 3-/6-month views, group by Monday-based weeks but include only the dates inside the selected page's calendar months. The first/last weekly buckets may therefore be partial. For August–October, the week starting Monday 27 July includes only 1–2 August in that page. Hover on a partial week shows its actually included date range. Do not include July values in the August–October total or count the same recorded day in both adjacent pages. [L1] [L5]
9. Render however many Monday-based weekly buckets are needed to cover the complete 3-/6-month page, rather than assuming exactly 13/26 buckets regardless of calendar alignment. Daily/monthly buckets likewise follow actual calendar boundaries. Primary and secondary series share the same page and bucket boundaries; a secondary series lacking activity does not remove a primary-series page. [L4] [L5] [L8]
10. Derive the settled date range, chart values, total, and average from the same selected page. The visible range describes included calendar dates through the last included day, not merely the start of the last weekly/monthly bucket. Retain metric definitions, units, rounding, palettes, and the existing average labels: Week `Daily Avg:`, Month `Weekly Avg:`, 3-/6-/12-month and Year to Date `Monthly Avg:`, All Time `Annual Avg:`. Count zero-valued included buckets/periods consistently with those meanings; a clipped preceding-month Monday must not create an extra month in a 3-/6-month average. [L5] [L6] [L8]
11. In Month, the hover title includes the weekday without a comma or year, for example **`Thu 15 Oct`**, using the `EEE d MMM` display shape. Preserve existing tooltip metric rows and values. Use the shared formatting boundary rather than parallel per-card date formatting. [L1]

### Eligibility, refresh, and calendar rollover

21. As a defensive safeguard, all three timeline cards, for every preset and both series, exclude Track dates after Dashboard's Mac-local reference calendar day from aggregation and first/last endpoint selection. This retains the agreed display cutoff, not an importer-validation rule or an assumption that the walk's location and the Mac always have the same current day. Compare intended calendar days using the Track-date decoding contract in Technical Decision 2; do not mistake the UTC representation of a stored Track date for a different calendar day. Preserve the complete page's remaining future dates as zero-valued buckets, with the existing average meanings and Year to Date elapsed-month behavior. Excluded Tracks become eligible when their calendar day arrives and the reference date is reconciled. If all otherwise usable Tracks are future-dated relative to that reference, use the existing no-qualifying-data state. This display safeguard itself performs no data repair and does not alter Latest Walk's selection rules; the separate approved recalculation below owns date corrections.
22. On Track-data refresh, keep the displayed calendar page whenever it remains within the permitted range. Recalculate its chart, range, total, average, endpoint destinations, and button states from the refreshed data. If removing records places the selected page before the new earliest permitted page, clamp to that earliest page. If no qualifying records remain, show the existing empty-dataset state. Adding records does not automatically move a card that previously used first or last. Apply calendar-rollover reconciliation when the reference date has also changed; stale movement, inspection state, or callbacks must not restore the superseded page.
23. Reconcile the reference calendar date on the next Dashboard activation, data refresh, or timeline interaction; no background midnight timer is required. When advancing the reference date changes a page sequence, a card that displayed the current page follows the new current page. A card displaying history selects the newly aligned page containing its previous page's last included calendar day, subject to the refreshed history bounds. For example, an October 2026 Last 3 Months current page of August–October becomes September–November in November; a historical May–July page becomes June–August because that newly aligned page contains 31 July. Historical Week/Month pages remain on the same calendar period when still permitted; All Time follows the same current-year-anchored reconciliation rule at a year boundary. A different preset always opens its current-period page under requirement 4. Reconcile selection, range, chart, summaries, navigation controls, and inspection state together, cancelling superseded movement. A reference-day change also updates future-date eligibility even when the page sequence itself is unchanged. Each card's browsing state remains independent.

### Location-derived Track dates and existing-data recalculation

24. Derive the Track's calendar day from the coordinates and valid, unambiguous timestamp of its first recorded GPX point. Preserve the importer's first-point selection convention: the first `trkpt` in the first `trkseg`, falling back to the first `rtept`. Resolve the geographic timezone at that point, then apply that timezone's rules at the recorded instant, including historical daylight-saving transitions, before extracting year/month/day. Use the stored original `gpxFile` for existing Tracks. The result must not depend on the importing/current Mac's timezone or a fixed Australian Eastern offset. Do not substitute a later point, the file-modification date, or the Mac's timezone when the required inputs cannot be resolved. [R1] [R2]
25. Future imports persist this location-derived day as `GpxTrack.trackDate`. Existing Tracks are recalculated from their stored GPX data under the same derivation rules. For every successfully resolved Track, update its linked `PeaksBagged.date` values to that same calendar day, preserving Track IDs, bagged-row IDs, associations, peak counts, and first-climb meanings. Subsequent peak-ascent synchronization must use the canonical Track day rather than recreate the old Australian Eastern/start-timestamp fallback. Update the Track date and linked dates atomically through the data-access boundary, and refresh affected projections consistently. Do not rewrite GPX content or start/end timestamps. [R1]
26. Corrected/new dates are calendar-only values: their year/month/day survive ObjectBox round-trips, serialization, formatting, aggregation, endpoint selection, and host-timezone changes. Their display/consumers must not reinterpret them as instants with `toLocal()`. Persist sufficient derivation/migration status to distinguish corrected dates from skipped legacy representations; do not infer this distinction from `isUtc` or a midnight-looking value alone. Technical Decision 2 defines the corrected encoding and the legacy compatibility boundary. [R1] [R2]
27. If GPX time is missing/invalid/ambiguous, coordinates are unusable, the GPX cannot be read, or geographic timezone/rule resolution fails, do not invent a walk day. For an existing Track, preserve its existing Track date and linked peak-ascent dates unchanged and report it as skipped with its identity and an actionable reason. For a future import, allow the import with `trackDate = null`, keep linked peak-ascent dates unknown, and show a visible warning explaining why the date could not be determined. Prevent subsequent synchronization from silently supplying a timestamp, file-date, Mac-local, or Australian Eastern fallback. Retain the ordinary unknown-date display and metric-eligibility behavior. [R2]
28. At the next app launch for an existing database without the completed date-recalculation version, automatically run the recalculation with visible progress. Show a completion summary with updated, unchanged, and skipped Track counts and skipped-record reasons; unchanged means the canonical date and linked dates already agree. Report operational failures visibly with diagnostic logging and an actionable retry path. Only publish successful completion after the database processing and completion metadata are durably committed; expose a coherent refreshed result to consumers. Timezone lookup/GPX processing occurs outside ObjectBox transactions, with required writes inside the data-access boundary. [R3]
29. Use a stable, durable per-database migration ID/version and completion marker. A reported unresolvable Track counts as a processed skip; skips do not trigger automatic recalculation on every launch. An interruption or operational/persistence failure leaves the migration incomplete and safely retryable on retry/next launch: rerunning must not duplicate records, damage associations, or partially update a Track and its linked dates. A completed database skips the recalculation on subsequent launches; fresh empty databases can be marked current immediately. Restoring/opening an older database without the marker must still trigger its recalculation. [R3]
30. Keep the location-based derivation permanently for future imports. Retain the small, version-gated existing-data migration after completion so older databases/backups remain supported. Remove that temporary scanning/orchestration code only in a later, separately scoped cleanup after establishing that all supported databases have passed the migration; retain the durable version metadata. Do not remove migration code automatically after the first successful run or equate one app installation with one database. [R3]

## Technical Decisions

1. Adopt the project-local **ADS-1.0** standard in `ai_specs/coding-rules.md` and **Desktop wide only** profile in `ai_specs/app-profile.md`: macOS-only, wide desktop, maximised on launch, keyboard/pointer affordances, semantic light/dark styling, accessible labels/focus and disabled feedback. ObjectBox, Riverpod, and GoRouter remain the mandatory foundations. No iOS, touch-layout, or compact-navigation requirements are introduced.
2. Preserve the glossary distinction between a `Track`, its calendar-only `Track date`, and a start timestamp. Convert `LatestWalkSummary`'s start timestamp at the display boundary and reuse `lib/core/date_formatters.dart`; keep Track-date formatters component-preserving. Encode corrected/new Track dates and linked peak-ascent dates as UTC-midnight calendar carriers, `DateTime.utc(year, month, day)`, in the existing ObjectBox `dateUtc` fields: UTC here is a stable encoding, not the walk's midnight instant. Decode these dates by their stored year/month/day, never by `toLocal()`. Audit affected Track/peak-ascent consumers, including timelines, My Ascents, and year grouping, so changing the host timezone cannot shift a corrected day. The current importer instead constructs Mac-local midnight, which ObjectBox returns as a UTC instant; the current timeline calls `toLocal()` and peak-ascent derivation separately applies Australian Eastern rules. Those are legacy representations/behaviors to replace through requirements 24–30, not approval to extract UTC components from unrecalculated legacy values. Persist status distinguishing successfully corrected/new dates from skipped legacy values; preserve skipped values and their legacy interpretation without claiming to recover an unavailable original day. Use the approved concrete metadata design in Technical Decision 10, preserving ObjectBox schema/model identity and using supported generation for entity changes. [L2] [R1] [R2] [R3] [R5]
3. Extend the shared `SummaryCardService` / `SummaryCard` / `SummaryChart` path so the three metric adapters receive identical page calculation and navigation behavior. Use calendar arithmetic and end-exclusive boundaries for selection/aggregation; a fixed number of elapsed 24-hour durations is not a safe definition of a local calendar week or month across DST. Keep metric derivation separate from page selection. [L1] [L4] [L5]
4. Maintain one authoritative selected-page state and derive its date range, button enablement, buckets, summaries, and scroll destination. Pixel offset alone is not the period's identity. Follow the standard's Riverpod/application-state and transient-presentation responsibility boundaries; retain existing provider-fed track data and explicit callback seams without a second persisted or widget-owned copy of domain data. Each card's selection remains independent. [L6] [L8] [L10]
5. Give scroll controllers/listeners, animations, hover/pinned selections, and scheduled callbacks explicit owners and disposal behavior. Invalidate superseded movement and callbacks when a newer page request, preset, refreshed dataset, or reconciled reference date takes effect. Reconcile or clear obsolete inspection state when the page/data changes, prevent stale tooltip values, and avoid post-disposal updates. The existing changed `summary_chart.dart` and tooltip tests are implementation evidence to reconcile, not authorization to overwrite unrelated work. [L8]
6. Reuse deterministic inputs already available: `SummaryCardService.buildTimeline(... now:)`, card/`DashboardScreen.now`, metric definitions, `LatestWalkSummary`'s pure selectors/formatting, and existing Riverpod test overrides. Add an injectable Dashboard reference clock through Riverpod, retaining explicit `now` inputs for focused tests. Production reads the Mac's local clock for Dashboard's reference date and Latest Walk timestamp display; location-derived Track days use the recorded location's timezone instead. The reference date is reconciled at the events in requirement 23. Wire this clock through the routed Dashboard path so robot journeys can override it with a fixed, advanceable value; extend the existing robot harness as needed rather than leaving its routed screen on `DateTime.now()`. Run the timezone-sensitive focused tests and full suite under `Australia/Hobart`, and the location-date regressions under multiple explicit host timezones as specified below. [L2] [L8] [R1] [R4]
7. The profile still marks minimum usable macOS width/height, supported resize range, native full-screen choice, and feature-specific wide-layout thresholds as **Pending**. This Spec invents no dimensions, breakpoint, or native full-screen policy. Date/page logic may proceed independently. Resolve a Pending value before any implementation or acceptance criterion that actually depends on it; if the one-row toolbar cannot satisfy applicable accessibility/usability constraints, report the conflict rather than silently wrap it or treat observed native dimensions as approved. [L9]
8. Own geographic timezone lookup, timestamp-to-location-day derivation, and recalculation orchestration behind injected service/provider boundaries. Use offline geographic timezone data and historical IANA timezone rules to retain the no-runtime-network contract; a timezone-rules package alone does not resolve coordinates to a timezone. Use the approved dependencies, provenance, licensing, coverage, and packaging in Technical Decision 9. Resolve the actual recorded location, including non-Australian walks; do not approximate by longitude, current UTC offset, or the Mac's settings. Replace lookup/data/persistence dependencies deterministically in tests. Keep startup orchestration app-owned with explicit lifecycle/retry ownership; widgets only render progress/outcomes and dispatch retry. [R1] [R2] [R3] [R5]
9. **Approved offline stack:** initially select `timezone_finder: 1.0.0` for coordinate-to-IANA-zone lookup and `timezone: 0.11.2` for timestamp conversion with historical rules; preserve reproducible application resolution in `pubspec.lock`. Initialize rules from `package:timezone/data/latest_all.dart`, not the reduced/default or ten-year dataset. The initial geographic data is Timezone Boundary Builder **2026e**, sourced from OpenStreetMap's worldwide land polygons; the initial rules are IANA tzdb **2026e**. Use the packages' embedded Dart data compiled into the macOS app, with no runtime download or external-machine dataset dependency. `timezone_finder` code is MIT, its boundary data is ODbL, and `timezone` code is BSD-2-Clause; document provenance and include the required OpenStreetMap/Timezone Boundary Builder attribution and data-license information. The approved geographic dataset simplifies borders to approximately 110 metres, has documented coastal coverage and deterministic overlap handling, and does not reconstruct historical geographic boundary movements; historical timestamp offsets come from IANA rules for the resolved zone. Uncovered locations follow requirement 27. The geographic package is newly released: retain independent geographic fixtures rather than treating package self-consistency as proof. Keep the adapter behind an injected `TrackDateDerivationService`; the package API takes longitude before latitude, so its boundary must deliberately map the GPX coordinate order. [R1] [R2] [R4] [R5]
10. **Approved ObjectBox metadata:** add `GpxTrack.trackDateStatus` with stable persisted integer codes `0` = legacy/unprocessed, `1` = resolved, `2` = legacy skipped, and `3` = unresolved new import; add `trackDateDerivationVersion` (`0` for unprocessed legacy data, initially `1` after processing), `trackDateTimezoneId` for a resolved IANA identifier when available, and `trackDateFailureReason` for a stable reason code translated into actionable feedback. Status `1` identifies component-preserving canonical dates; status `2` preserves the existing Track and linked dates and their legacy interpretation; status `3` keeps a new import's Track and linked dates unknown. Add a dedicated singleton `TrackDateMigrationMetadata` with `id = 1`, stable migration key `track_location_calendar_day`, `completedVersion` initially targeting `1`, completion timestamp, geographic and IANA dataset versions, updated/unchanged/skipped counts, and serialized skipped-record identities/reasons. An absent completion record is incomplete; a successfully committed empty database may be marked current with zero counts. This record belongs to the ObjectBox database and travels with its backups, rather than relying on installation-wide SharedPreferences flags. Follow the existing database-owned metadata pattern in `lib/models/route_graph_import_metadata.dart`; retain existing entity/property identities and regenerate with `dart run build_runner build --delete-conflicting-outputs`, reviewing `lib/objectbox-model.json` and `lib/objectbox.g.dart` together. 11. **Approved recalculation commit boundary:** prepare GPX/timezone outcomes outside ObjectBox transactions, coordinate competing Track/ascent writers during the operation, then commit the Track-date changes, linked ascent-date changes, per-Track status, migration outcome summary, and completion marker in one final ObjectBox write transaction through the data-access boundary. Preserve all existing Track IDs, bagged-row IDs, relationships, GPX content, timestamps, and skipped dates. Count a successfully resolved Track as unchanged when its canonical date and linked dates already agree, even if its metadata needs recording. Run/coordinate the date migration before incompatible Track/ascent backfills or writers, and expose progress, completion, skipped reasons, failure and retry through app-owned Riverpod state.
